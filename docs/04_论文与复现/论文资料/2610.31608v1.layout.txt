                                               Humanoid mobility over complex terrain requires accurate foot placement and coordinated whole-body motion
                                               capable of robust adaptation. Reinforcement learning (RL) policies trained without motion references generalize
arXiv:2610.31608v1 [cs.RO] 22 Oct 2026




                                               well across terrains, yet eliciting effective whole-body coordination through reward design and exploration alone
                                               remains challenging. In contrast, reference-based methods yield natural motions but struggle to adapt beyond
                                               the predefined trajectories in their training data. We introduce a learning framework that bridges these two
                                               approaches by using motion data strictly as a training prior. An explicit, self-supervised, attention-based contact
                                               planner predicts future body–environment contacts, while a contact-conditioned tracker translates these plans
                                               into coordinated whole-body motion. Together, they establish contact as an interface between sensory observations
                                               and control actions. This abstraction allows the policy to internalize the contact-aware structure of locomotion
                                               rather than directly replicating kinematics. Consequently, a single policy can exploit data-driven motion priors
                                               for natural whole-body coordination while retaining the flexibility to discover environment-dependent behaviors
                                               through reinforcement learning. Building on the learned contact planner and tracker, a subsequent fine-tuning
                                               stage bootstraps their acquired capabilities to explore environment variations beyond those covered by the original
                                               motion data. Operating entirely from onboard observations without any reference inputs at deployment, this
                                               framework enables versatile humanoid mobility in challenging environments.

the dataset. Training follows a backward curriculum: the robot is initialized at progressively earlier states along the reference trajectory, advanc-
ing to an earlier state only after the current one has been successfully learned. R P , R R , Rv , Rω , RC , R j,p , and R j,v denote the position, rotation,
linear velocity, angular velocity, contact, joint position, and joint velocity tracking reward, respectively. (C) Stage 2: attention-based contact
planner. The planner predicts contact plans by attending to map observations, conditioned on robot proprioception and the goal command. It
is trained in a supervised manner using ground-truth contact labels from a motion buffer containing rollout trajectories generated by the whole-
body controller. The tracker is co-trained using the predicted contacts from the planner.

Fig. 3. Overview, part II. Stage 3: RL fine-tuning. Training combines reference tracking on motions sampled from the demonstration dataset
(left) with goal-reaching rollouts on procedurally generated terrains outside the demonstration distribution (right). In the goal-reaching envi-
ronment, the contact planner predicts contact plans from map scans, robot proprioception, and the goal command; the whole-body controller
executes these plans and is fine-tuned with RL using task (Rt ) and contact-tracking (RC ) rewards. Successful rollouts are stored in the fine-
tuning buffer and subsequently used as additional supervision for the contact planner, forming a bootstrapping loop in which planner-generated
contacts are executed, validated through successful interaction, and recycled to improve the planner. Reference trajectories are sampled propor-
tionally from the original dataset during fine-tuning to retain the previously learned tracking behaviors.

Fig. 8. Overview of the LiDAR-based neural mapping pipeline for height map observations. (A) At each frame, LiDAR points are projected
into x-y grid cells and the highest point per cell is retained, producing a 2D local grid map. A neural network then predicts local elevations with
associated uncertainty, and the predictions are fused into the global map using odometry. (B) The network uses CNNs to predict per-grid logits
over three fill modes: the exact measured value, the neighborhood average, or leaving the grid empty. The mode with the highest logit determines
the grid’s elevation prediction, while the disagreement among modes and logits yields the uncertainty estimate. (C) The network is trained on
synthetic data generated from diverse scene meshes.
